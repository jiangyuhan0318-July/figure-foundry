# Scientific schematics and method diagrams

## Meaning before layout

Read the relevant method or mechanism description. Identify inputs, operations, outputs, grouping, shared components, and any training-only or hypothetical relationships. State what every arrow means: flow, dependency, transformation, association, or a supported causal relation. Preserve direction and uncertainty.

For a redraw, keep a small fidelity record of content preserved, merged, or omitted. Do not invent a module to fill whitespace or remove a necessary branch just to meet a node budget.

## Select the working format

- SVG is a useful default for editable vector diagrams and paper insertion. Use native text and geometry.
- Use native editable PPTX objects when the user requests PowerPoint editing. A bitmap pasted into a slide is not an editable reconstruction.
- HTML with inline SVG is useful for browser presentation or requested interaction. It is not compulsory for a static paper figure.
- Image-generation concept exploration is optional when it helps a conceptual illustration and the tools are available. Keep data plots, measured images, and exact structures source-derived. Do not claim an unexposed model version or require a paid backend just to draw a normal diagram.

## Visual grammar

Use shape, containment, alignment, or stacking to express meaningful distinctions. Accent the focal mechanism while keeping supporting components quiet. For three data categories, the three saved palette colors can retain distinct roles; the editorial preference for few accents is not a categorical-data limit.

Give connectors clear endpoints and avoid routing them through unrelated nodes. Prefer simple orthogonal paths for box-based workflows; use curves or diagonals when the relationship or geometry benefits. These connector preferences do not constrain numerical lines or scientific geometric structures.

Place labels beside a connector with a clean gap or background mask. Use distinct attachment points for multiple edges when needed. Keep arrowheads and labels readable at final size. Put long explanation in the caption rather than squeezing it into every node.

For a complex method, organize an overview and a local detail inset or another figure. Preserve the requested content; there is no universal nine-node ceiling or mandatory three-stage pipeline.

## Rendering and checks

Use a complete drawing scene with one source of geometry, colors, and labels. Provide a meaningful SVG title/description when practical. Avoid external font/image dependencies in an offline deliverable.

Inspect the exported figure, not just the source. Check label fit, line crossings, arrow direction, grouping, training/inference semantics, clipping, and readability. Verify that edits remain possible in the advertised source format. Deliver the editable source and requested exports with a caption and actual QA results; do not impose a fixed number of refinement rounds.
